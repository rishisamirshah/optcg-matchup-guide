② The MIRROR (Nami vs Nami) — your key matchup

Headline

GO SECOND IF YOU CAN, AND PLAY THE LONG GAME. Second seat wins the mirror 53.6% (436/813), and the edge grows with game length: 47.4% in short games (<=12 plies, n=116) but 60.1% in long games (>=17 plies, n=278). The mirror is a grind decided after own-turn 9, not an early race. Whoever pokes first life usually LOSES (first-life dealer wins only 45.2%, n=812); the receiver wins. Win by out-developing (winner deploys a wider board and out-draws the loser) then bursting life from turn 9 onward, NOT by early aggression.

why_second_wins:

  • Structural last-attack: the second-seat player owns the final attack turn of the game in 423/812 games vs 389 for first seat — in a life race the player who swings last on the lethal turn closes it.
  • Second seat wins MORE both ways: via concede 299 vs 256, via life-out 136 vs 121 (n=812). The edge is not a concede artifact.
  • Going second draws on turn 1 (first player skips), and the mirror's median length is 14-15 plies, so that extra card compounds over a long game.
  • Raw engine output is nearly identical between seats (first vs second: deploys 6.96 vs 6.92, char-attacks 9.31 vs 9.66, draws 7.38 vs 7.29, counters 7.89 vs 7.86, all n=813). The second-seat edge is positional, not from doing more — so do NOT try to 'out-tempo' from the first seat.

practical: At the die roll: choose to go SECOND. If you are forced first, you are slightly behind (46.4%) — your best out is to keep the game SHORT and decisive (first seat is break-even at 47.4% second-WR in <=12-ply games), i.e. press a real life lead rather than letting it grind to turn 17+ where second seat hits 60%.

The First Life Trap

claim: Dealing first life correlates with LOSING. Do not spend early tempo just to poke a life off.

first_life_dealer_overall_wr: 45.2

n: 812

n_games_no_first_life_taken: 1

detail:

  • When the FIRST seat deals first life (happens in 568/812 = 70% of games, almost always on their own-turn 2), the first seat wins only 44.0% — the receiver (second seat) wins 56.0%.
  • When the SECOND seat deals first life (n=244), the second seat wins 48.0% — the receiver (first seat) wins 52.0%.
  • First life is dealt on the dealer's own-turn 2 in 494/812 games (median own-turn 2, mean 2.92). It is an opening-tempo poke into an empty board.
  • Early aggression is anti-predictive: in the 546 games with an early-life lead, the player ahead on life through own-turns 1-4 won only 42.9% (234/546). Life taken in own-turns 1-4 is actually slightly HIGHER for the eventual loser (mean 0.75) than the winner (mean 0.64).

mechanism: A turn-2 leader swing for 1 life trades your DON/tempo and (because of Nami's draw-on-life-loss) hands the opponent a fresh card. Against a mirror that defends with +2000 and recurs life to hand, one early life means little and the tempo you spent could have developed your board. The opponent answers by building, and out-develops you.

practical: In the early game (your turns 1-4) prioritize DEVELOPING your board and engine over poking the opposing leader. Take a free life only when it costs you nothing (e.g. an attack that would otherwise do nothing). Do not rest DON or commit a swing solely to deal early life.

Step By Step Plan

  • phase: Pre-game / die roll

    step: Choose to go SECOND.

    stat: Second seat wins 53.6% (436/813); the edge is 60.1% in long (>=17-ply) games (n=278). Going first you are at 46.4%.

  • phase: Mulligan

    step: Keep hands that develop a board and run the engine over hands that only enable an early leader poke. You want bodies + your draw/recursion pieces (EB03-055 Nico Robin at 99.2% inclusion, EB03-053 Nami at 97.5%, OP11-106 Zeus 95.8%, EB04-058 Borsalino 89.1% are near-universal staples).

    stat: Winners out-draw losers 8.0 vs 6.7 engine draws/game and out-attack with characters 10.7 vs 8.3 (n=813). The engine + board, not the leader, win the mirror.

  • phase: Own-turns 1-4 (early)

    step: DEVELOP. Drop bodies (first body around own-turn 3) and grow your engine. Do NOT spend DON or a swing just to deal first life into an empty board.

    stat: First-life dealer wins only 45.2% (n=812); early-life leader wins only 42.9% (n=546). Losers deal slightly more early life (T1-4 mean 0.75 vs winners 0.64) and play more early counter value (1303 vs 1170 in T1-5).

  • phase: Own-turns 1-4 (defense)

    step: Bank your counters. Don't over-defend the early pokes — take a poke or two and save counter cards (OP11-106 Zeus = 2000 counter, OP12-112 Baby 5 = 2000, EB04-058/EB03-053 = 1000 counter) for the turns that actually matter.

    stat: The LOSER plays more counters over the game (8.15 vs 7.6) and more total counter value (12162 vs 11268, n=813). Out-spending counters early is a losing pattern; the winner is the one dictating, not reacting.

  • phase: Own-turns 5-7 (mid)

    step: WIN THE BOARD. This is where the board gap is widest — get to 2-3 characters that stick and start swinging with the board, not just the leader. Keep your own life topped via life-to-hand / add-to-life recursion.

    stat: Winner board width T5 1.99 / T7 2.96 vs loser 1.71 / 2.72 (n>=370). Winner life actually recovers T5 2.85 -> T8 3.12. The +2000 buff is mandatory but NOT an edge (both buff ~5x; winner-more in only 318 games vs loser-more 329).

  • phase: Own-turns 9-11 (close)

    step: BURST. Convert your wider board into a sustained multi-attack life push. Land your big finisher (OP13-042 Whitebeard SP, 10c/12000) — winners get it down 88.8% vs 79.3%. Force the opponent to spend their whole hand on counters.

    stat: Life-taken jumps at own-turn 9: winner 0.47/turn vs loser 0.30, holding through T11 (0.44 vs 0.22). Winners take ~5 life total vs losers ~3 (medians 5 vs 3).

  • phase: Closing

    step: Don't wait to be ahead on life to commit — a dominant board closes from even or behind. Push the moment the opponent is out of counters; many opponents concede at 2-3 life once the board is lost.

    stat: Winners close from a median of 2 life (38 won from 0). Losers concede at 0-1 life in 345/556 games, but 199 conceded at 2-3 life with the board lost.

Pivotal Decisions

  • id: 1

    decision: Take the second seat at the die roll.

    stat: 53.6% second-seat WR overall (n=813), 60.1% in long games (n=278). First-seat is only break-even (47.4%) in fast <=12-ply games (n=116).

    why: Structural: second seat owns the last attack (423 vs 389 final-turn ownership) and draws turn 1. Engine output is otherwise identical between seats.

  • id: 2

    decision: Do NOT spend tempo to deal first life. Develop instead.

    stat: First-life dealer wins 45.2% (n=812); early-life (T1-4) leader wins 42.9% (n=546). Dealing first life off the first seat -> you win only 44.0%.

    why: An early poke trades your DON and gives the mirror a free card (draw-on-life-loss). The receiver out-develops and wins.

  • id: 3

    decision: Attack with your BOARD, not just the leader. Build bodies that stick and swing every turn.

    stat: +2.45 character attacks/game for winners (10.71 vs 8.26, n=813) — the largest behavioral gap. Total deploy count is identical (~7), so the edge is bodies SURVIVING and re-attacking, not deploying more.

    why: Leader attacks are equal (6.83 vs 6.79). The character board is the entire difference in offensive output.

  • id: 4

    decision: Keep the card engine running wider than your opponent.

    stat: +1.3 engine draws/game for winners (7.99 vs 6.68, n=813).

    why: More cards = more counters held AND more board, the two things that win the grind. The deck's whole identity is the draw engine.

  • id: 5

    decision: Bank counters early; don't out-spend the opponent defending the early pokes.

    stat: Losers play MORE counters (8.15 vs 7.6) and more value (12162 vs 11268; and 1303 vs 1170 in T1-5). Winner spends LESS on defense.

    why: If you're burning counters to stop turn-2 pokes you're playing the loser's game. Take the poke, save the card for the turn-9+ burst when it stops a lethal swing.

  • id: 6

    decision: Time your life push for own-turn 9+, after winning the board (turns 5-7).

    stat: Through T8 both players take ~0.2-0.3 life/turn; at T9 winners jump to 0.47 vs 0.30 and hold (n>=755).

    why: Life taken before T9 is non-predictive. The decisive damage is dealt late off an established board. Patience is the line.

  • id: 7

    decision: Land your big finisher (OP13-042 Edward Newgate SP, 10c/12000).

    stat: Deployed by the winner in 88.8% of games vs 79.3% by the loser (n=813).

    why: A 12000 body that the opponent must double-block or eat is the cleanest way to force life through in the burst phase. 79% inclusion across mirror lists.

  • id: 8

    decision: If forced to go FIRST, keep the game short and press a real life lead; don't let it grind to turn 17+.

    stat: First seat is break-even in <=12-ply games (second-WR 47.4%, n=116) but collapses to 39.9% (second-WR 60.1%) in >=17-ply games (n=278).

    why: The second seat's positional edge compounds with length. Your only first-seat path is a faster, more decisive game where your tempo matters.
